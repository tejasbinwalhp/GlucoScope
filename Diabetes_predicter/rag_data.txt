GLUCOAI / GLUCOSCOPE KNOWLEDGE BASE

PURPOSE AND LIMITS
GlucoAI is an educational assistant for general diabetes screening and healthy-habit questions. It does not diagnose diabetes, estimate a person’s clinical risk, interpret symptoms, or replace a clinician. A website model result is not a laboratory result. Do not start, stop, or change medicine based on chatbot advice. For urgent or concerning symptoms, contact local medical services or a qualified healthcare professional.

WHAT A GLUCOSCOPE RESULT MEANS
The GlucoScope result is generated by a machine-learning model using a historical Pima dataset. It reflects the model’s estimated probability of a positive label for the values entered and the model’s training context. It is not an individual’s validated probability of developing diabetes, a diagnosis, or a substitute for testing. Dataset population and limitations mean it may not generalize to every person or population. Discuss concerns with a clinician, who can decide whether testing is appropriate.

SCREENING AND TESTS
Diabetes screening and diagnosis should be based on validated laboratory tests interpreted by a healthcare professional, such as HbA1c, fasting plasma glucose, or an oral glucose tolerance test. A clinician determines which test is appropriate and whether a result needs confirmation. Do not use a chatbot or online estimate to diagnose yourself.

FOOD: PRACTICAL GENERAL GUIDANCE
A varied eating pattern can include non-starchy vegetables, pulses and beans, whole or minimally processed grains, whole fruit, nuts or seeds, and suitable protein sources. Choose water or unsweetened drinks more often than sugary drinks, and limit foods high in added sugar and heavily processed snack foods where practical. Portions and meal plans depend on a person’s health, medicines, culture, budget, and preferences; a clinician or registered dietitian can tailor advice. No single food cures or prevents diabetes.

MOVEMENT AND DAILY HABITS
Regular movement supports general health. Adults can work toward about 150 minutes per week of moderate activity, adjusted to ability and medical advice; begin gradually if inactive. Sleep needs vary, and consistent, adequate sleep supports wellbeing. Avoid presenting lifestyle habits as a replacement for prescribed treatment or clinical follow-up.

UNDERSTANDING RISK FACTORS
Family history, age, prior gestational diabetes, and some health measurements can be relevant to diabetes risk, but no single factor proves that someone has diabetes. Body-mass index and waist measurements are imperfect screening context and should not be interpreted alone. A clinician can consider personal history and appropriate tests.

WHEN TO SEEK PROFESSIONAL HELP
Ask a qualified healthcare professional about screening if you have concerns, a strong family history, or risk factors. Seek timely medical advice for symptoms such as unusual thirst, frequent urination, unexplained weight loss, or blurred vision; these symptoms have multiple possible causes and do not establish a diagnosis.

CHATBOT RESPONSE RULES
Use calm, plain language. Distinguish a GlucoScope estimate from a clinical test. Never claim the estimate is a diagnosis or validated personal risk. Do not invent medical facts, numerical risk cutoffs, or personalized treatment advice. If a question is not covered by this knowledge base, say so and direct the user to a qualified healthcare professional.

READING A DIABETES OR HEALTH REPORT
Start with the test name, measured value, unit, reference interval, and collection conditions (for example, fasting or after a meal). Reference intervals can differ between laboratories and tests. A single out-of-range flag does not by itself establish a diagnosis; discuss the result, symptoms, medicines, and history with a healthcare professional. GlucoScope's percentage is a model output, not a value from a lab report.

COMMON GLUCOSE REPORT ENTRIES
Fasting plasma glucose (FPG) is measured after a fasting period specified by the clinician or laboratory. HbA1c (also called A1C) estimates average glucose over roughly the prior 2-3 months, but some conditions can affect its accuracy. The oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT) measures glucose response after a standardized glucose drink. A random plasma glucose is interpreted with symptoms and clinical context. These tests use different methods and decision criteria; do not compare their numbers as if they were interchangeable. Ask the ordering clinician to explain the report's units, reference range, and whether repeat testing is needed.

HOW TO READ A CBC OR OTHER REPORT
A CBC includes entries such as haemoglobin, white blood cells, and platelets; these are not direct diabetes measurements. A chemistry panel may include electrolytes, kidney and liver markers, and glucose. Lipid reports commonly list total cholesterol, LDL, HDL, and triglycerides. Each result should be read with its unit and that laboratory's reference interval. GlucoAI cannot diagnose from isolated report values; share the complete report with the clinician who ordered it, especially if a result is flagged or you have symptoms.

BMI: FORMULA AND ADULT CATEGORIES
For adults, BMI = weight in kilograms divided by height in metres squared. For example, 70 kg and 1.75 m gives BMI 22.9 kg/m2 (70 / 1.75^2). Common adult international categories are below 18.5 underweight, 18.5 to below 25 healthy-weight range, 25 to below 30 overweight, and 30 or above obesity. Some Asian populations can have health risks at lower BMI values and use lower action thresholds. Categories are screening guides, not diagnoses or direct measures of body fat or health. The calculator on this site uses the common adult categories and is not suitable for interpreting children, pregnancy, or individual clinical risk.

BMI: PRACTICAL NEXT STEPS
A BMI outside a reference category is not a judgment and does not identify the cause of a health concern. Consider discussing it with a healthcare professional alongside waist measurement, blood pressure, laboratory results, medical history, and personal goals. If a change is appropriate, gradual and sustainable habits around meals, movement, sleep, and follow-up are generally more useful than crash diets. Seek tailored guidance from a clinician or registered dietitian, especially with chronic illness, pregnancy, a history of disordered eating, or medicines that affect weight.

BMI AND DIABETES
BMI alone cannot tell whether someone has diabetes or predict an individual's future. Diabetes can occur at any body size. A clinician uses health history and validated laboratory tests to assess glucose status. Do not use BMI or this website's calculator to delay screening or medical care.

ENTERING GLUCOSCOPE INPUTS
The estimate form uses four values: 2-hour plasma glucose in mg/dL, diastolic (lower-number) blood pressure in mmHg, BMI in kg/m2, and age in completed years. Enter the matching measurement and unit only. Do not substitute HbA1c, fasting glucose, random glucose, systolic blood pressure, or the full blood-pressure reading; these are not interchangeable inputs. If you do not know a value or your report uses a different test or unit, do not guess. The model does not use family history, medicines, symptoms, or other health factors, so its output cannot account for them. The historical dataset represents a limited population and its recorded ages range from 21 to 81; estimates may be especially unreliable outside that represented age range.

UNDERSTANDING THE GLUCOSCOPE PERCENTAGE
The percentage is the model's estimated probability of a positive label in its historical training data for the values entered. It is not a measured glucose percentage, a laboratory result, a diagnosis, or a clinically validated personal chance of developing diabetes. In this app, below 30% is labelled lower, 30% to below 60% middle, and 60% or above higher. These are software display groups only, not medical thresholds or actions. A lower result does not rule out diabetes, and a higher result does not confirm it. For any band, focus on general sustainable habits and discuss personal screening with a qualified healthcare professional. At higher display bands, arrange a timely discussion about validated testing; do not make treatment changes based on the model.
